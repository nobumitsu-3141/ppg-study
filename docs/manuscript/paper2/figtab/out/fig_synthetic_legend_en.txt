Figure 4. Three fits superimposed on one synthetic beat (illustration, not PWDB): position of the second component relative to the peak of the synthetic reflected wave.

What is shown: rows are synthetic beats (top: type-3-like, reflected-wave arrival parameter 0.10 s, overlapping systole; bottom: type-1-like, arrival parameter 0.28 s, separated; reservoir time constant 0.45 s in both), columns are the fit variants of table 8 ((0) frozen; (6b) truncated at 0.65 of the beat length T; (9) residual taken in the first-derivative domain). Each panel shows the fitted signal (normalised synthetic beat, thick black line), component 1 (short grey dashes, open circle at its peak), component 2 (colour and line style per column: (0) solid, (6b) long dashes, (9) dash-dot; marker at its peak) and the peaks of the synthetic forward and reflected waves (two dotted lines). In the truncation column the region beyond 0.65T is hatched (not fitted). T, beat length. The panel title gives ΔT = peak of component 2 − peak of component 1, and the true value = peak of the synthetic reflected wave − peak of the forward wave (the spacing of the two dotted lines).

The arrival parameter (0.10 and 0.28 s) and the true value (78.0 and 310.0 ms) differ because, in the generator, the width and skewness of the reflected wave change with its arrival, and the true value is defined as a difference of peaks, not of the position parameters (docstring of synth_beat in script 50; lab-log entry 141).

**The numbers in the panel titles come from the synthetic beat only, not from the 4,374 PWDB subjects.** The reading drawn from the synthetic beats, that the lower bound on the position of the frozen fit's second component (Δμ ≥ 0.08 s) was the main cause (lab-log entry 141), was refuted when the 4,374 PWDB subjects were refitted: not a single beat sat at that bound (entry 144; note to table 8). Even in the synthetic type-3-like beats, all 7 beats at the edge of the search range had the second component's skewness at its upper limit, and only 2 of the 7 were at the Δμ bound (continuation of entry 144). The figure illustrates where the two versions that exclude the diastolic decline from the fit place the second component; it does not establish the mechanism.
